You hear it before you see it. A low, continuous sound that at first seems to come from everywhere, and then resolves into a direction once the ground starts to fall away.

## The wrong kind of light

I arrived in the middle of a grey afternoon. Flat light, no shadow, the kind that makes every photograph look like a photocopy of itself. The obvious move was to leave and come back at dawn.

I stayed, mostly because leaving meant walking back up the slope I had just slid down.

## What an hour of weather teaches

Standing still in bad light is a good exercise. The scene does not change for you, so you stop waiting for it to and start looking at it instead.

Things I noticed in the hour:

- The spray does not fall where the water does. It drifts sideways, and it is always colder than the air.
- The river has two sounds: the crash where it drops, and a lower, almost continuous rumble underneath.
- Everything within three metres is wet, including the notebook I had been carefully not dropping.

The light did eventually break. Not dramatically — a thin band of it crossed the far bank for maybe four minutes and then went out. Enough.

## Notes from the edge

I have no larger point about patience. The water was loud, the rocks were slippery, and for a while there was nothing else to attend to, which is rarer than it should be.

I took the picture on the way back up, from the wrong side of the river, and it is better than anything I got from the edge.
