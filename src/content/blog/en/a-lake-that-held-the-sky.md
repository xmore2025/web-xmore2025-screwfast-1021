The road up was not on the plan. It was one of those signs with a name I could not pronounce and a distance short enough to feel like a dare, so I turned.

## Forty minutes of nothing but switchbacks

For the first half hour there was no view at all — just a wall of rock on one side and a drop on the other, and the engine working harder than it wanted to. I nearly turned around twice. The second time I decided that turning around would take as long as continuing, which is a bad reason to keep going and, in this case, the right one.

Then the trees opened.

## The lake

It sits in a bowl of green ridges with mountains behind it, and on the day I arrived the water was doing the one thing water rarely does: nothing. No wind, no chop. The whole sky was in it, clouds and all, slightly darker than the real thing.

I sat on a rock for a long time. There is not much to report. A bird made a sound like a hinge. Somewhere below, a boat that never moved.

> The photograph is the least interesting part of the afternoon, but it is the part I can show you.

## What the detour was worth

I have been trying to explain to myself why an hour of sitting still felt more useful than the afternoon of driving it replaced. The best answer I have is that nothing was being optimised. No route, no schedule, no purpose beyond being somewhere I had not been.

The lake does not care whether I came. That turns out to be a relief.
