There is a stretch of time after dinner when the day has stopped asking for anything. The emails have been answered or abandoned. The kitchen is mostly clean. Nobody needs a decision.

## The best hour is the unclaimed one

I have started protecting this hour more carefully than I protect my working hours, which would sound indulgent if it were not so cheap. It costs nothing. It only requires not filling it.

What happens in it is unremarkable. A cup, a chair, a lamp. Sometimes a book that I read three pages of. Occasionally I get up and put something away and sit back down, which is the entire event.

## Small rituals are load-bearing

The decorations on the table are left over from something — I have never been sure whether they are early or late. They stay because taking them down would require a decision, and this is the hour in which decisions are not made.

That is the whole argument, really. A house is not a project. Some of it is allowed to simply persist, slightly out of season, and be the thing your eye lands on while you are thinking about nothing.

## Closing the day

I used to end evenings by reviewing them. What got done, what did not, what tomorrow would need. It is a good habit that quietly ruins the hour it is applied to.

Now the review happens in the morning, when it is useful. The evening is left alone, and it turns out there is not much to review anyway: a quiet hour does not produce anything you could report.

Which, I think, is the point of it.
